WHAT A GOOD READING LOOKS LIKE
- Exactly 2 sentences, 170 to 280 characters in total. A third sentence is never allowed.
- Sentence 1 names one concrete thing from the tradition and what it traditionally means. It must be accurate.
- Sentence 2 turns that into one specific, small, believable action or nudge for today, matched to the mood and the focus area, and the reading ends on that action itself. Name a real detail (a message you have not answered, a meeting, a purchase you keep postponing, a tight shoulder, a walk), never an abstraction.
- One idea per reading. No lists of three. No closing aphorism, moral or summary after the action. Rejected endings look like: "Plain warmth lands better than polish.", "Small daily care builds real stamina.", "Good ideas deserve real numbers.", "Quiet transitions rarely announce themselves."
- Tone follows the mood. stressed or tired: slower, practical, permission to do less. confused: clarifying, offers one way to sort things. peaceful: quiet, observational. hopeful: warm but not gushing. excited: energetic, turns momentum into one concrete step.
- Use "tends to", "points to", "often", "is read as". Never promise outcomes.
- Vary how readings begin. At most two readings in a batch start with the same word. At most three start with "You".
